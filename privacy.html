What Market List keeps, and where

No accounts, no ads, no tracking. The short version: a list lives in one place online and anyone with its link can open it; a few features send what you give them to an AI service to be read; nothing is sold.

Your list

Each list is one record in Google's Firebase database (Cloud Firestore), filed under the list's code — the long string of letters and digits in its link. Anyone who has the link can open and change the list; there is no sign-in, so keep the link to the people you shop with (up to four).

In the list, shared with everyone on it:

  • the items, their amounts, notes and which shop they are for; Regulars; the meal plan and recipes;
  • the names people chose when they joined (whatever they typed — it need not be a real name);
  • when things were bought, so the app can suggest restocks; the shop order it has learned;
  • prices and spending read from receipts — the shop and its suburb, the day, each item's price and the total; never the photo;
  • saved occasions; each shop's aisle map; your memberships' settings (which ones you have, and when this month's 10% was used);
  • "who is shopping now", only if that person turned it on: their name, the shop's name and the time, for 90 minutes.
  • who is on each list (since v2.44): the accounts allowed to open it, the names they chose, and the list's current link key. A removed person's phone can no longer open the list.

Only on this phone: which lists it knows, the name you chose, your settings and look, the list's history, a daily backup for a week, what it has learned for its predictions, the membership cards you add (their numbers and photos), the last shop's ticked items (kept two days, to match its receipt against), a log of the times the screen got stuck (sent only if you report it), and small counters (receipts read today).

With your account, if you add your email: Firebase (Google) keeps your email, and your password only in scrambled form (the app never stores it), to sign you in and to send a sign-in or password-reset email when you ask for one. Your account keeps which lists are yours — their codes and names, and the time you left one — so a new phone can get them back. Never the lists' contents. Signing out leaves it; to delete the account, use Settings › Your data › Your account › Delete my account: your email, password and saved lists go, and your name comes off the lists it was on.

What leaves the app, and only when you use it

These features go through the app's own small server (on Vercel), which holds the keys to the services below. Your list is never sent with them.

  • Smart add — the words you type go to an AI model (Google Gemini, or Groq) to be split into items.
  • A recipe from text, a photo, a web page or a TikTok — the text or photo goes to Gemini or Groq; for a link, the server fetches that page (for TikTok, the video's public caption) and sends its text.
  • Recipe search — what you search for goes to a web-search service (Tavily, Serper or Brave, whichever is set up) and the results to Gemini or Groq.
  • Scanning a membership card — if the phone can read the card's barcode itself, the photo stays on the phone. Otherwise the photo goes to Gemini or Groq to read the card's name and number. The app's server never keeps or logs the photo or the number; the card itself is then kept on this phone only.
  • Reading an aisle sign (mapping a shop's aisles) — the photo goes to Gemini or Groq to read the aisle's number and what the sign lists. The server never keeps or logs the photo or what it read.
  • Reading a receipt — the photo goes to Gemini or Groq to be read. The app's server never keeps or logs the photo or what it read; the prices you confirm are saved in your list.
  • Shared prices (only if you turn it on, v2.47) — the prices you confirm from receipts at Coles, Woolworths and ALDI: the chain, the item's name, the unit price and the day, stored in Firebase under your account's random id so you can update your own. Other households that turned it on can read them; they see the price and that id, never your email, list, shop, suburb or card.
  • Report a problem — what you write, and, if you leave "Include app details" ticked, the app's version, the page, your layout, icon and theme settings, screen size and browser. It becomes an issue in the developer's GitHub repository (or a line in the server's log).
  • Crash reports — when the app breaks, it sends the error's first lines, the version, the page, the screen size and the browser: at most three a day, never the list, never its code.
  • A quiet account (from v2.39, once it is switched on) — each phone signs in to Firebase (Google) with an anonymous account: a random id, no name, no email. The phone sends it with the features above so the server can tell one phone from another. It is not linked to you unless you add your email (below). "Forget everything on this phone" leaves a quiet account behind unused, and signs an email account out of this phone (the account keeps which lists are yours).
  • Daily counts — so nobody can run up the AI bill, the server counts how many times a day each phone uses these features (or, before a phone is signed in, each internet connection, kept only as a scrambled code). The count is kept by Upstash, a database service, and forgotten after a day. Nothing else about the request is kept.

Each of these services handles what it receives under its own terms — Google, Groq, the search services, GitHub, Upstash, and Vercel, which also sees the usual web requests (an IP address and the time) as it serves the app. What they keep, and for how long, is set by them and could not be checked from here.

App Check

To stop other programs from using the app's servers, the app asks Google's reCAPTCHA (Google Cloud Fraud Defense) to vouch that it is the real app. reCAPTCHA looks at the device and the browser — never your list — and sends that to Google. It has been on since version 2.52.

Daily limits

So that nobody can use the AI features without limit, the app's server counts how many times each signed-in phone used each of them today (for example "3 receipts"). The count is kept in the app's database under the phone's anonymous account number — numbers only, never what was read — one record per day.

What the app never does

  • No advertising, analytics, tracking pixels or third-party cookies.
  • Nothing is sold or shared for marketing.
  • No location leaves the phone: the shops' distances are worked out on it.

Deleting

In Settings › Your data:

  • Leave this list — your name comes off it and this phone forgets it. The others keep the list.
  • Empty this list for everyone — items, Regulars, the plan, prices, spending, occasions and what the app has learned are cleared for everyone on it. Its name, people, categories and stores stay.
  • Forget everything on this phone — every list, name, setting and backup kept here. The shared lists stay for their people.

"Clear prices and spending" in Settings › Prices clears just those. A problem report already sent can be removed by the developer — ask through Report a problem.

Questions

Use Settings › Report a problem. It reaches the person who builds the app.
